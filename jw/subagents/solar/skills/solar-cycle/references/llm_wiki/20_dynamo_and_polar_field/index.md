# Module 20 — Dynamo and Polar-Field Physics

## Purpose

Ground mechanism explanations and polar-precursor hypotheses in a bounded
physical picture rather than post-hoc statistical storytelling.

## Required knowledge families

- Solar-dynamo problem and the poloidal/toroidal distinction.
- Differential rotation and the omega effect.
- Babcock–Leighton source process.
- Active-region tilt, decay, surface flux transport, and cross-equatorial
  cancellation.
- Meridional circulation and turbulent transport.
- Polar-field reversal and rebuilding.
- Polar field near minimum as a possible following-cycle precursor.
- Stochastic emergence, hemispheric asymmetry, and unresolved deep-dynamo
  components.

## Questions this module must answer

1. What causal chain connects surface active regions to polar-field reversal?
2. Which link is observationally constrained and which remains model-dependent?
3. Why can a polar precursor be physically meaningful but statistically weak?
4. Which other variables can weaken a one-precursor interpretation?
5. What evidence would discriminate a transport explanation from an accidental
   cross-cycle correlation?

## Boundaries

- Babcock–Leighton is a framework with variants, not a fully closed description
  of the solar dynamo.
- The polar field is a constraint on a poloidal component, not a complete
  measurement of the internal dynamo state.
- The short direct-observation record limits the confidence of Cycle 26 claims.

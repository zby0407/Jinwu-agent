# Module 10 — Solar Cycle State and Morphology

## Purpose

Provide the cycle-scale vocabulary used by Cycle 26 assessment, historical
backtests, mechanism interpretation, and self-correction.

## Required knowledge families

- Approximately 11-year sunspot cycle versus 22-year Hale magnetic cycle.
- Cycle minimum, rising phase, maximum, declining phase, and cycle boundary.
- Amplitude, length, rise time, decline time, rise slope, and integrated
  activity.
- Waldmeier-effect definitions and their sensitivity to cycle construction.
- Long-term modulation and why the available number of cycles is small.
- North–south phase and amplitude asymmetry.

## Questions this module must answer

1. Which quantities define cycle strength and morphology?
2. Which quantities are observations and which are derived features?
3. How are cycle boundaries established, and how does boundary uncertainty
   propagate?
4. What does the Waldmeier effect state, and what would count as a violation?
5. What is the relation between the 11-year observational cycle and the Hale
   magnetic cycle?
6. Which historical-cycle comparisons are scientifically meaningful?

## Boundaries

- The cycle is quasi-periodic, not a clock.
- A cycle-level association is not automatically a dynamo mechanism.
- Cycle 26 remains a future target; only bounded trend statements are legal.
- Short-term flare activity cannot define the long-term cycle on its own.

## structure

## Hypothesis statement

The strength of the Sun's polar magnetic field near the minimum of solar cycle N is positively correlated with the peak amplitude of solar cycle N+1.

## Physical motivation

In the Babcock-Leighton dynamo, the polar field at minimum represents the poloidal seed field that will be sheared by differential rotation into the toroidal field of the next cycle. A stronger seed field is expected to produce a stronger toroidal field and therefore a more active next cycle.

## Required data

- Polar magnetic field strength (or proxy) near cycle minimum.
- Peak sunspot number of the subsequent cycle.

## Testable prediction

If the polar field at the minimum preceding cycle N+1 is above/below the historical average, cycle N+1's peak amplitude is likely to be above/below average.

## Counter-conditions / limitations

- Polar-field measurements cover only a few cycles (since ~1970s), limiting statistical power.
- The relationship may be nonlinear or affected by meridional circulation speed.
- Other dynamo ingredients (tilt angles, flux emergence latitude) also matter.
- A single precursor should not be treated as a deterministic forecast.
